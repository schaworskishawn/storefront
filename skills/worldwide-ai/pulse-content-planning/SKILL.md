# Pulse content planning

## Use this when

- An approved campaign needs a schedule of posts drafted.

## Do not use this when

- What the function needs has not been supplied: it reports what is missing and creates nothing, so gather it first.
- The request belongs to another agent's function: it goes through Nexus, not through this procedure.
- The request is to act outside the workspace (send, post, pay, file, or change a live record): this procedure only prepares and stores a result.

## What you need

- Campaign (required)
- Approved brief (required)
- From (required)
- To (required)
- Channels (required)
- Supported facts (required)
- Approved assets available

## Procedure

1. Read the campaign dates, the channels listed, the facts and the approved assets.
2. Place each post on a date inside the campaign.
3. Write each caption from the supplied facts only.
4. Attach only assets from the approved list.
5. List any idea left out because no fact or asset supported it.

## What you produce

- A stored ContentCalendarDraft record, checked against its declared format before it is kept.
- It waits for a person to accept or reject it.
- Its sources, anything missing, assumptions and warnings are listed with it.

## Checks before you finish

- Every date is inside the campaign and every channel is one that was listed.
- Every asset is from the approved list.
- No health claim, and nothing aimed at people under the legal age.

## When something goes wrong

- If the facts are too thin for the number of posts asked for, draft fewer and say why.

## Example

**Request:** "Pulse: draft content calendar."

**Result:** A content calendar draft is stored with dated entries per channel, each caption's supporting facts, and the asset it uses or needs, waiting for a person's review.

## Limits

- It drafts. No post is scheduled or published, and a caption states only what a supplied fact supports.
- Drafts a calendar for review. Never schedules or publishes a post.
- Nothing is published. Channel performance data isn't connected.
- The wording comes from a model and is checked by code. A person reviews every draft before it is used.
