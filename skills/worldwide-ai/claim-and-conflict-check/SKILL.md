# Claim and conflict check

## Use this when

- A draft, brief or review package is about to be stored and contains statements a reader will rely on.
- Several supplied records describe the same thing and may not agree.

## Do not use this when

- The result is purely a calculation whose method and inputs are already shown line by line.

## What you need

- The result as drafted.
- The evidence set it was drafted from.

## Procedure

1. List each statement a reader could act on: figures, dates, eligibility, commitments, product facts.
2. For each, point to the supplied item or approved source that supports it. A statement with none is unsupported.
3. Label each as a recorded fact, an estimate with its basis, or a recommendation.
4. Where two records disagree, show both values and where each came from.
5. Remove or flag unsupported statements. Do not soften them into hedged wording that still reads as fact.

## What you produce

- The result with every statement supported, labelled or removed.
- A list of conflicts and of what could not be established.

## Checks before you finish

- No figure appears that is absent from what was supplied or calculated.
- Every cited item is one that was actually supplied.
- Uncertainty is stated in words, never replaced by a confidence score.

## When something goes wrong

- If the central claim has no support, the result says so plainly instead of being written around the gap.
- If a conflict can't be resolved from the evidence, it is left for a person with both sides shown.

## Example

**Request:** A proposal draft states "delivery in 5 days" and the supplied terms say "7 to 10 business days".

**Result:** The draft is corrected to the supplied terms, and the removed claim is listed as unsupported with the reason.

## Limits

- It checks statements against supplied evidence. It cannot establish whether the evidence itself is true.
