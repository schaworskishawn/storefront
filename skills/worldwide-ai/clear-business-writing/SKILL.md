# Clear business writing

## Use this when

- A function produces prose: a reply, a proposal, copy, a brief, a summary.

## Do not use this when

- The output is a table of calculated figures with no prose.

## What you need

- The facts to convey.
- The audience and the purpose.
- The brand voice notes, where approved ones exist.

## Procedure

1. Lead with what the reader most needs, then the next step, then the detail.
2. Use the exact product names, quantities and units from the supplied facts.
3. Keep to what the evidence supports. Leave a claim out rather than soften it.
4. Match the tone to the reader without changing the facts.
5. For this business: no health, cessation or safety claims, and nothing that appeals to people under the legal age.

## What you produce

- Text that is accurate, short enough to be read, and clear about the next step.

## Checks before you finish

- No date, price, deadline or commitment appears that was not supplied.
- No health, cessation or safety claim appears.
- Any claim left out for lack of evidence is listed for the reviewer.

## When something goes wrong

- If the facts supplied are too thin to write something useful, say what is missing instead of padding.

## Example

**Request:** "Write a short product description" with a brief that gives capacity and nicotine strength but nothing about flavour notes.

**Result:** A description stating capacity and strength as given. Flavour notes are not invented, and are listed as missing.

## Limits

- A draft is saved for a person. Nothing is sent or published.
