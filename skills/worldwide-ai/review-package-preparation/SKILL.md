# Review package preparation

## Use this when

- A function's result is a draft, a proposal or a workpaper that a person must accept or reject.

## Do not use this when

- The result is a verified calculation that changes nothing and needs no decision.

## What you need

- The result.
- Its sources, assumptions, warnings and what is missing.

## Procedure

1. Open with the decision being asked for, in one sentence.
2. Show the evidence beside each part it supports.
3. State what would change if it is accepted, and what stays the same.
4. List what is missing, what was assumed, and any warning, before the reader has to look for them.
5. Tie the package to the exact stored version by its checksum, so the decision is about what was read.

## What you produce

- A stored result waiting for review, with its sources, gaps, assumptions and warnings visible.

## Checks before you finish

- Nothing in the package reads as already done. A proposal is described as a proposal.
- The reviewer is a person, and is never the agent that produced the result.
- The decision is recorded with who made it and when.

## When something goes wrong

- If the result changed after it was read, the decision does not apply to the new version.
- If essential evidence is missing, the package says the decision can't safely be made yet.

## Example

**Request:** A tax workpaper for the quarter, with two lines left unresolved because no rule was in effect.

**Result:** The package asks: accept this workpaper as prepared? It shows each line's rule, and lists the two unresolved lines first.

## Limits

- Accepting a result records a decision. It does not send, post, file or pay anything.
