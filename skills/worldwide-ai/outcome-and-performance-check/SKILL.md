# Outcome and performance check

## Use this when

- A task is about to be marked complete or handed to review.
- Someone asks how well an agent or a procedure is doing.

## Do not use this when

- The only evidence is that a model said the work was done: that is not evidence.

## What you need

- The task and its stored result.
- Review decisions, failures, timings and usage recorded for the period.

## Procedure

1. Read the stored result back and recompute its checksum.
2. Confirm it belongs to this task, this function and this agent, and is tied to the input supplied.
3. Mark the task only after those checks pass. If they can't be completed, the outcome is unknown.
4. When reporting performance, give counts with their denominators: accepted out of reviewed, failed out of run.
5. Say what the figures do not cover, and call a small sample inconclusive.

## What you produce

- A task in a state its evidence supports.
- Performance figures with denominators and coverage.

## Checks before you finish

- No task is complete without a stored, verified result.
- No improvement is claimed from a figure without a comparison on the same cases.
- Demonstration runs are left out of business counts.

## When something goes wrong

- If the stored result doesn't match what was produced, the task is marked outcome unknown and a person is told to check before relying on it.

## Example

**Request:** "How is Relay doing this month?"

**Result:** 12 drafts reviewed: 9 accepted, 2 edited, 1 rejected. 3 failed runs out of 15. Twelve is a small sample: no trend is claimed.

## Limits

- Reporting quality cannot complete a task or establish a business benefit by itself.
