**Quick answer:** peel off the damaged wrap, keep (or replace) the insulator ring on the positive end, slide on a new 18650 heat-shrink wrap so it overhangs both ends evenly, then heat it all round with a heat gun on low or a hair dryer until it shrinks tight over both shoulders. It takes about two minutes. Don't re-wrap a battery that's dented, swollen, leaking or hot: recycle it.

## Why a torn wrap matters

An 18650 battery's metal can is its negative terminal. Only the thin plastic wrap, and a small insulator ring on the positive end, keep the can apart from the positive contact. If the wrap is nicked near the top, the positive contact in a mod or charger can touch the bare can and short the battery. A shorted lithium-ion battery can get very hot, vent or catch fire, so a damaged wrap is a reason to stop using the battery until it's re-wrapped.

## How to check your batteries

Look at each battery every time you charge it, and re-wrap it before you use it again if you find:

- a split, nick or tear, especially around the top edge
- a wrap that's peeling, loose or has shrunk back from the ends
- a missing or damaged insulator ring around the positive button

## What you need

- one [18650 battery wrap](/product/18650-battery-wrappers) per battery: pre-cut PVC heat-shrink, about 29–30 mm flat and 70–72 mm long
- a heat gun on its low setting, or a hair dryer on its hottest setting
- a new insulator ring, if the old one is missing or damaged

## How to re-wrap an 18650 battery, step by step

1. **Remove the old wrap.** Peel it off with a fingernail. Don't cut it with a metal blade against the battery.
2. **Inspect the battery.** If the can is dented, swollen, leaking, rusty or warm, stop and recycle it.
3. **Check the insulator ring.** Leave a good one in place on the positive end. Fit a new one if it's missing or torn.
4. **Slide on the new wrap.** Centre it so the same amount overhangs each end.
5. **Shrink it.** Heat the wrap evenly, turning the battery as you go, until it's tight along the body and folds over both shoulders. Keep the heat moving and never use an open flame.
6. **Let it cool and check it.** The wrap should be smooth and tight with no gaps at either end, with the positive button and the flat negative base still exposed.

## When not to re-wrap

A new wrap fixes the insulation, not the battery. Recycle the battery instead if it's dented, swollen, leaking or rusty, gets hot while charging or in use, or no longer holds a charge. Take old lithium-ion batteries to a battery recycling drop-off rather than the household bin.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I use a hair dryer instead of a heat gun?** Usually, yes. A hair dryer on its hottest setting will shrink thin PVC wraps, just more slowly than a heat gun on low. Don't use a lighter or any open flame.

**Do 18650 wraps fit 20700 or 21700 batteries?** No. They're sized for 18 mm batteries. 20700 and 21700 batteries are wider and need their own wraps. Shorter 18 mm batteries, such as the 18350 and 18500, can use an 18650 wrap trimmed to length.

**How often should I re-wrap?** Whenever the wrap is damaged. Batteries carried loose in a pocket or bag get nicked most, so carry spares in a plastic battery case, away from keys and coins.
